# S09 — Kim Nguyen, 33, vendor election-day help desk

**Snapshot:** Vendor support specialist. On election day she staffs the phone line for
dozens of counties: poll workers and county technicians call when a device misbehaves.

**Context:** Starts at 4 a.m.; peak call volume at opening (6–7:30) and closing. She
can't see the device — she works from what a stressed, often non-technical caller
reads to her. Multiple product versions in the field.

**Goals:** Diagnose fast over the phone, get the device back in service or tell them to
use the backup procedure, log the incident accurately.

**Knowledge:** Product — expert; per-county configurations — partial. Election
procedure — good.

**Language & literacy:** English and Vietnamese; some callers are bilingual poll workers.

**Abilities & assistive tech:** Headset; dual monitors with knowledge base and ticketing.

**Devices & modes:** Knowledge base, incident tracker, remote logs where available —
and the caller's reading of the screen.

**Behaviours:** Asks "what does the screen say exactly?" first. Relies on message IDs
and screen names to find the right procedure.

**What breaks for them:**
- Messages without a short, stable, speakable ID (e.g. "Code P-112") — or with long hex
  codes the caller can't read aloud correctly.
- Screens without a visible name or step number, so the caller can't say where they are.
- Different wording for the same problem across devices/versions.
- No visible software version / election definition identifier on a status screen.
- Callers who can't tell whether the issue is their action or a device fault (7.3-K).
- Recovery procedures that need steps poll workers aren't allowed or trained to do.

**Walkthrough tasks:** Over a simulated phone call with S01, diagnose a "ballot not
accepted" scanner state and a BMD that won't print, using only what S01 reads aloud.

**Recruiting profile:** Vendor or county help-desk staff with at least one election day
of phone support. Not in 8.4-A — but their needs shape every poll-worker message.
