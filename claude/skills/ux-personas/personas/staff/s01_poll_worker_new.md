# S01 — Gloria Mendez, 66, first-time poll worker

**Snapshot:** Retired school administrator, volunteered after a neighbour asked. One
3-hour training two weeks ago; a printed binder; works a 14-hour day.

**Context:** Arrives at 5:30 a.m. to set up before polls open at 7. Noisy gym, long
line by 7:05, a precinct lead who is busy elsewhere. Bilingual (English/Spanish), so
she's often asked to help Spanish-speaking voters.

**Goals:** Open on time, not make a mistake that "ruins the election", help voters
without seeing their choices, close out correctly when exhausted.

**Knowledge:** Election procedure — minimal, from training. Tech — moderate (smartphone,
office software). Doesn't know the device's internals or jargon.

**Language & literacy:** English and Spanish native, good reader.

**Abilities & assistive tech:** Reading glasses (forgets them); some knee pain
(crouching at low device ports is hard).

**Devices & modes:** Poll-worker screens on the BMD and scanner (opening, ballot
activation, accessibility setup, closing reports), seals and logs on paper.

**Behaviours:** Follows on-screen prompts literally. Afraid to press anything not
mentioned in training. When a message is unclear, calls the help line or waits for the
lead.

**What breaks for them:**
- Setup steps without confirmation that each worked; she can't tell if the device is
  ready (7.3-O: workers shouldn't have to guess setup is correct).
- Technical or coded messages ("ERR 0x2F", "Election definition hash mismatch")
  instead of what happened and what to do (7.3-O, 7.3-K, 7.3-P).
- Turning on accessibility features (audio, ATI, language, contrast) for a voter
  requires steps she wasn't trained on (8.4-A tasks b, g).
- Selecting the correct ballot style at a vote center: similar style names, no
  confirmation of the voter's precinct (8.4-A task f).
- Screens where she can see a voter's selections while helping (6.1 privacy).
- End-of-day closing: long sequence, irreversible steps without clear warning.

**Walkthrough tasks:** Open the polls; activate a ballot for a Spanish-speaking voter at
a vote center; set up audio and the keypad for a blind voter; recover from a paper jam;
close the polls and print reports. (8.4-A tasks 1a–g.)

**Recruiting profile:** First- or second-time poll workers with standard jurisdiction
training. 8.4-A: range of experience — low end.
