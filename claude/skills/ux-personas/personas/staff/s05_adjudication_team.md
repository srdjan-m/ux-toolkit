# S05 — Rosa Alvarez & Jim Park, central-count adjudication team

**Snapshot:** A bipartisan pair (one from each major party), temporary staff hired for
canvass. They review ballots the scanner flagged — overvotes, faint or ambiguous marks,
write-ins — and decide voter intent under state rules.

**Context:** Hundreds of ballot images per shift, side by side at one screen, observers
watching over their shoulders. Every decision is logged and may be challenged.

**Goals:** Decide each ballot correctly and consistently, agree as a pair, keep the
pace without fatigue errors.

**Knowledge:** State voter-intent rules from a half-day training (examples binder).
Tech — moderate (Rosa), low (Jim, 70).

**Language & literacy:** English native; occasional write-ins in other scripts.

**Abilities & assistive tech:** Jim: cataracts, prefers larger images and high contrast.
Both: eye strain after hours of looking at scans.

**Devices & modes:** Adjudication workstation: ballot image with flagged marks, contest
by contest decision controls; large monitor; mouse and keyboard.

**Behaviours:** Zoom into the mark, compare with the voter's other marks on the same
ballot, discuss, decide. Speed up as the shift goes on; fatigue makes them accept the
system's suggestion.

**What breaks for them:**
- The system's suggested interpretation shown so prominently that it biases the
  decision (default effect — NN/g power-of-defaults).
- Small or blurry ballot images; no zoom; no contrast control (relevant to Jim).
- Decision controls far from the image region they refer to (proximity), or that
  look alike for different outcomes.
- Can't see why a ballot was flagged (which rule, which contest).
- No easy undo/review of a decision just made; no log of who decided what.
- Overvote vs undervote vs write-in status shown by colour only (7.1-F).
- Observers able to see decisions but not understand them (transparency).

**Walkthrough tasks:** Adjudicate 10 flagged ballots incl. hesitation marks, an X instead
of a filled oval, a crossed-out choice, and a write-in that matches a listed candidate.
Reverse one decision.

**Recruiting profile:** Canvass/adjudication board members, mixed ages, bipartisan.
Not in 8.4-A — central count is separate. Ties to the ballot mark recognition project.
