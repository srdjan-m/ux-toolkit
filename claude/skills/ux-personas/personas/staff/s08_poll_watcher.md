# S08 — Raymond Ellis, 60, partisan poll watcher

**Snapshot:** Retired insurance adjuster, appointed by a political party to observe at
polling places and at central count. Not election staff: he may watch and challenge
through official channels, but must not touch equipment or talk to voters (state law
sets the exact rights and distances).

**Context:** Stands at a marked distance behind poll workers or behind a barrier at
central count, notebook in hand. Observes opening, the day, closing, and adjudication.

**Goals:** See that procedures are followed and counts make sense; document anything
irregular; be able to explain later what he saw.

**Knowledge:** Procedure — moderate, from party training. Tech — moderate. Starts
suspicious of anything he can't see or understand.

**Language & literacy:** English native.

**Abilities & assistive tech:** Distance vision fine, near vision needs glasses —
reading screens from 2–3 m matters.

**Devices & modes:** None of his own on the system; he reads public-facing displays:
the scanner's public counter, poll-worker screens turned towards observers,
adjudication screens, printed zero and results tapes posted at the door.

**Behaviours:** Counts things himself and compares. Notes every error message he sees.
Interprets unexplained beeps, codes or screen changes as possible problems.

**What breaks for them:**
- Public counters and status too small or low contrast to read from the observer
  position; no clear "ballots counted" vs "voters checked in" distinction.
- Coded or scary messages visible to observers ("FATAL", "OVERRIDE") for routine events
  (7.3-K, 7.3-O plain messages).
- Adjudication screens where he can see that a decision was made but not why
  (rule, mark, contest).
- Zero report and results tapes that are hard to read or compare (terms differ from
  screens, abbreviations).
- Any display that lets him — or anyone near — see a voter's selections (6.1 privacy).

**Walkthrough tasks:** From 2.5 m, read the scanner's public count at opening and closing;
watch a paper jam recovery and decide if anything looked wrong; watch five adjudications
and write down why each was decided.

**Recruiting profile:** Experienced poll watchers/observers from more than one party.
Not in 8.4-A — but tests perceived integrity of public-facing screens.
