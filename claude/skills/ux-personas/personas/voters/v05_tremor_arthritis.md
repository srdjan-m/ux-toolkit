# V05 — Walter Kowalski, 71, essential tremor and arthritis

**Snapshot:** Retired machinist. Hand tremor worsens under stress; arthritis in his
fingers. Doesn't consider himself disabled and won't ask for accessibility features.

**Context:** Votes at his precinct, often at busy times. Uses a touchscreen like
everyone else.

**Goals:** Get his votes right on the first try without the embarrassment of
corrections.

**Knowledge:** Civic — moderate. Tech — low-moderate; uses a flip phone and a tablet
for photos.

**Language & literacy:** English native.

**Abilities & assistive tech:** Taps land several mm off target; sometimes double-taps;
can't do precise drags or long-presses reliably. Would use a physical keypad if offered.

**Devices & modes:** BMD touchscreen, visual mode; physical keypad available but not
discovered.

**Behaviours:** Rests his palm on the screen edge (stray touches). Taps the wrong
candidate and then can't figure out how to deselect. Rushes when people are waiting.

**What breaks for them:**
- Small or crowded targets, insufficient gaps (7.2-I: 12.7 mm targets, 2.54 mm gaps).
- Actions on touch-down instead of release, so a slip can't be cancelled.
- Double-tap selecting then immediately deselecting with no visible state change.
- Unclear how to change a selection in a vote-for-one contest (tap other name? tap
  same name to clear?).
- Swipe or drag gestures, long-press, edge-of-screen controls (palm contact).
- Physical alternative (keypad) exists but nothing on screen mentions it.

**Walkthrough tasks:** Select a candidate, accidentally tap a neighbour, correct it.
Vote for 3 in a vote-for-3 contest. Cast quickly with a line behind him.

**Recruiting profile:** 65+, tremor or arthritis affecting fine motor control, doesn't
use AT. Counts toward 8.3-A 1d (limited dexterity, low-dexterity controls).
