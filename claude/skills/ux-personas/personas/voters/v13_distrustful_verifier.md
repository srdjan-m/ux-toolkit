# V13 — Ruth Kaminski, 63, distrustful verifier

**Snapshot:** Retired bookkeeper. Has read online claims about voting machines and is
sceptical. Votes in person specifically so she can check her own ballot.

**Context:** Takes her time; will ask poll workers pointed questions and may complain
publicly if something looks off.

**Goals:** See proof that what she chose is what's recorded and counted.

**Knowledge:** Civic — high. Tech — moderate; suspicious of anything "hidden".

**Language & literacy:** English native, careful reader.

**Abilities & assistive tech:** Reading glasses; otherwise none.

**Devices & modes:** BMD with printed paper ballot or VVPAT; or a hand-marked paper
ballot plus scanner.

**Behaviours:** Reads the printed record line by line against her choices. Notices any
mismatch in wording between screen and paper. Distrusts barcodes/QR codes she can't
read. Asks "how do I know it counted?"

**What breaks for them:**
- Printed record that differs in wording, order or abbreviation from the screen.
- Paper where the human-readable text is small, faint or truncated (7.1-I 3.5 mm,
  7.1-C contrast).
- Selections encoded only in a barcode/QR code with nothing explaining what it is.
- Vague confirmation copy ("Thank you!") instead of a clear statement of what happened
  ("Your ballot has been cast and will be counted").
- No clear way to spoil the printed ballot and start over if it's wrong (7.3-F).
- Status messages that sound like system faults or blame her (7.3-K).

**Walkthrough tasks:** Vote, compare the printed record against selections, find one
"wrong" item, spoil and revote, cast; or hand-mark, scan, and read the scanner's feedback.

**Recruiting profile:** Self-reported low trust in voting technology; votes in person.
General population for 8.3-A — important for perceived integrity.
