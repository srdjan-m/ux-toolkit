# V04 — Priya Raman, 34, quadriplegia (C5 spinal cord injury)

**Snapshot:** Policy analyst, power wheelchair user. Very limited hand function; uses a
sip-and-puff switch and voice control on her own devices.

**Context:** Comes with her own sip-and-puff device and needs the BMD to accept it
through the standard switch jack. A poll worker has to position the device for her.

**Goals:** Vote independently and privately with her own switch, without tiring out.

**Knowledge:** Civic — high. Tech — high: experienced with switch scanning.

**Language & literacy:** English native.

**Abilities & assistive tech:** No fine motor control; two-switch input (sip = select,
puff = next), or a single switch with auto-scanning. Fatigue builds with many presses.
Seated height and reach depend on the voting booth layout.

**Devices & modes:** BMD with two-switch (dual switch) input, visual mode; screen at
seated eye level.

**Behaviours:** Counts presses. Will abandon features that need long scanning loops.
Uses the review screen carefully because corrections are expensive.

**What breaks for them:**
- Any action reachable only by touch or drag; the whole flow must work with two
  switches (5.1-A, 7.2-A modes, limited/no-dexterity).
- Long scan orders: every non-essential focus stop (decorative elements, repeated
  instructions) costs presses. Count switch presses per contest as a finding metric.
- Write-in keyboard with no efficient scanning (row/column scanning).
- Timeouts that don't account for switch speed (7.2-O).
- Focus indicator too subtle to follow while scanning (WCAG 2.4.7, 2.4.11).
- Booth and device placement out of reach of a seated voter (Not checked: hardware).

**Walkthrough tasks:** With two switches: vote three contests, write in a name, review,
change one vote, cast. Count presses per task.

**Recruiting profile:** Uses switch, sip-and-puff or other no-dexterity input daily.
8.3-A 1d: visual interface with low and no dexterity controls.
