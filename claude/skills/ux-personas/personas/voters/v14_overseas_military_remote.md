# V14 — Staff Sgt. Danielle Brooks, 31, overseas military voter

**Snapshot:** US Air Force logistics NCO stationed in Germany, voting absentee for her
home county under UOCAVA (Uniformed and Overseas Citizens Absentee Voting Act). Uses
the county's remote ballot marking: she receives a blank ballot electronically, marks
it on her own device, prints it and returns it.

**Context:** Applied with the Federal Post Card Application (FPCA). Ballot arrives by
email link ~45 days before a federal election (MOVE Act). Works shifts; marks her
ballot on a personal laptop or base computer, prints on a shared base printer, mails
it through the military postal system — or uses fax/email return if her state allows
it. Time zone 6–9 hours ahead of the county; help lines are closed when she's free.

**Goals:** Vote correctly the first time, return it in time, know it arrived and was
accepted.

**Knowledge:** Civic — moderate; little knowledge of local contests back home. Tech —
high, but on locked-down government networks and unfamiliar printers.

**Language & literacy:** English native.

**Abilities & assistive tech:** None. (Other UOCAVA voters may be blind or low vision;
remote marking is often the only accessible absentee option — test it with V02/V03.)

**Devices & modes:** Her own browser (whatever the base machine has — possibly old, with
blocked scripts or downloads), a phone, a printer of unknown quality. Not a certified
polling-place device.

**Behaviours:** Does it in one sitting late at night. Reads return instructions twice.
Worried about missing a deadline and about postage/envelope rules.

**What breaks for them:**
- Site that fails on restricted browsers or blocks on plugins/downloads; no
  low-bandwidth path.
- Printed ballot unclear: which pages to print, which way up, whether it'll scan, where
  to sign; printouts that cut off margins or barcodes on A4 paper (European default) vs
  US Letter.
- Return instructions buried or legalistic; deadline stated without time zone; oath /
  declaration and envelope assembly steps not shown visually (7.3-N images).
- No way to know the ballot was received and accepted (H1 visibility of status).
- Session timeouts while she reads measures she doesn't know (7.2-O spirit).
- Accessibility of the remote marking tool itself (WCAG 2.2 AA at minimum; screen-reader
  support matters most here).

**Walkthrough tasks:** Open the link on an old browser, mark a ballot with local
measures, print on A4, assemble the return envelope from the instructions, find the
deadline in her time zone, check ballot status later.

**Recruiting profile:** UOCAVA voters (military or overseas civilians) who have voted
absentee in the last 4 years; include at least one with a disability. Not one of the
8.3-A polling-place groups — remote marking is tested separately, typically to WCAG.
Federal resource: FVAP (Federal Voting Assistance Program).
