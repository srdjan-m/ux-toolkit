# V01 — Jordan Reyes, 19, first-time voter

**Snapshot:** College sophomore, votes in person for the first time at a vote center
near campus. Fluent with phones, unfamiliar with how elections work.

**Context:** Between classes, 20 minutes to spare, a line behind them. Registered
through a campus drive; brought the wrong ID first time round.

**Goals:** Vote for the two races they care about, get through the rest without feeling
stupid, be sure it "counted".

**Knowledge:** Civic — low: doesn't know what a judicial retention question is, unsure
whether skipping a contest is allowed, never seen ranked-choice instructions. Tech —
high, but phone-shaped: expects swipe, auto-advance, instant feedback.

**Language & literacy:** English native, reads fast, skims instructions.

**Abilities & assistive tech:** None.

**Devices & modes:** Ballot-marking device (BMD) touchscreen, visual mode.

**Behaviours:** Skips instruction screens. Tries to swipe between contests. Taps
quickly and moves on. Leaves contests blank without realising undervotes are allowed
and fine. Wonders if the printed record is "the vote" or a receipt.

**What breaks for them:**
- Instructions only on a first screen they skip; needed help not at the point of need
  (7.3-N, NN/g recognition over recall).
- Jargon: "undervote", "retention", "straight-party", "write-in" without explanation
  (7.3-P plain language).
- Gestures that do nothing or do something unexpected (swipe) with no feedback.
- Ambiguity about skipping: is a blank contest an error? Undervote notice phrased as an
  error causes anxiety (7.3-K: say whose action, what they can do).
- Not knowing when the vote is actually cast vs only printed (H1 visibility of status).

**Walkthrough tasks:** Vote two contests, skip the rest, cast. Rank three candidates in a
ranked-choice contest. Change a selection after reaching the review screen.

**Recruiting profile:** 18–24, first or second election, no poll-worker experience,
daily smartphone user. Counts toward 8.3-A general population (English).
