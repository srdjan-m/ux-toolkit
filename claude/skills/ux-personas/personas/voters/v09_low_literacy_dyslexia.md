# V09 — Darnell Washington, 52, low literacy and dyslexia

**Snapshot:** Warehouse supervisor, sharp and well-informed from radio and TV, reads
slowly and with effort. Has hidden his reading difficulty for years.

**Context:** Votes in person at busy times after work. Would never ask for reading
help in public.

**Goals:** Vote the way he decided from the news, get through measures without being
tricked by wording, finish without looking slow.

**Knowledge:** Civic — moderate-high from audio/visual media. Tech — moderate.

**Language & literacy:** English native; reading at roughly a grade 4–5 level; long words
and dense paragraphs cause him to skip.

**Abilities & assistive tech:** Dyslexia: letter reversals, losing his place in long
lines, trouble with all-caps and justified text. Doesn't know that audio mode could
help him.

**Devices & modes:** BMD, visual mode. Would benefit from audio + visual together.

**Behaviours:** Recognises candidate names by shape and party. Reads the first few
words of measures and guesses. Uses visual cues (icons, colour, position) heavily.

**What breaks for them:**
- Dense legal text without a plain-language summary where the law allows one (7.3-P).
- ALL CAPS names and headings, justified text, long line lengths (ux-review
  typography.md).
- Instructions phrased negatively or with conditions last ("Do not … unless …") (7.3-P:
  condition first, say what to do).
- Similar-looking candidate names with no distinguishing layout (party, position).
- No discoverable audio-with-text option; audio only marketed as "for blind voters".
- Yes/No measure wording where "Yes" means "against something" — double negatives.

**Walkthrough tasks:** Vote a ballot with two long measures and two candidates with
similar names. Find and use audio + visual mode without asking.

**Recruiting profile:** Self-reported difficulty reading forms, or dyslexia; native
English. Counts as general population; also tests plain-language (7.3-P).
